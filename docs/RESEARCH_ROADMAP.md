# TransientPipeStateFinder research roadmap

> The purpose of our research is not simply to reconstruct a pipeline state. It is to determine how much measurement information is needed, how rapidly uncertainty loses its influence on future behavior, and whether the resulting predictions become sufficiently reliable in time to support an engineering decision.

## Research boundaries

- Reuse the existing `isothermal-pipe-sim` C++ forward solver.
- Do not create a replacement physics solver.
- Keep reconstruction, uncertainty analysis, optimization,
  and reporting in TransientPipeStateFinder.
- Use AGPL-3.0-only for the statefinder project, subject to
  dependency and redistribution review.
- Preserve discrete mass conservation.
- Distinguish physical imbalance from numerical mass defect.
- Reserve theta for the time-integration weighting parameter.
- Reserve p/P for pressure, not generic parameter vectors.
- Use bold xi for generic scenario parameters.
- Keep future boundary conditions fixed in current
  forecast-sensitivity experiments.
- Distinguish local linearized results from nonlinear guarantees.

## R5.1 Closure and R5.2 Experimental Validation

### R5.1 — Completed

The following capabilities are integrated:

- Constrained full-space state reconstruction.
- Discrete tangent-linear and adjoint operations.
- Measurement-information and identifiability analysis.
- Nonlinear forecast continuation.
- Engineering decision classification.
- Causal state qualification.
- Independent nonlinear restart validation.
- Integrated engineering assessment generation.

Baseline verification: 52/52 CTest tests passed.

The R5.1 demonstration uses synthetic measurements generated by the
same forward model, with all four measurement channels available.

The demonstration's early qualification time establishes workflow
correctness, not a measured physical uncertainty-decay timescale.

### R5.2 — Experimental Validation

The next milestone will investigate four questions.

#### A. Dynamical memory

How rapidly do perturbations in initial pressure and mass flow
influence subsequent transient predictions?

Evaluate nonlinear perturbation propagation, tangent-linear
predictions, conservation diagnostics, and error attenuation.

#### B. Measurement sufficiency

How does progressively assimilating measurements change the
identifiability and forecast accuracy of the reconstructed state?

Evaluate sparse measurement schedules, causal measurement prefixes,
information rank, unresolved directions, and nonlinear restarts.

#### C. Noise and uncertainty

How robust are qualification results to measurement noise,
incomplete observations, and imperfect reconstruction?

Use reproducible noise realizations and report empirical
qualification behavior without accessing future measurements.

#### D. Engineering decision sufficiency

Can a reconstructed state support reliable engineering decisions
even when some state directions remain poorly identified?

Distinguish full-state reconstruction accuracy from accuracy in
the engineering outputs that determine a decision.

### R5.2 Acceptance Requirements

- Preserve the validated R5.1 regression baseline.
- Reuse the existing forward solver directly.
- Prevent future-measurement leakage into causal reconstructions.
- Perturb both pressure and mass-flow initial conditions.
- Distinguish physical mass imbalance from numerical conservation error.
- Record exact source and experimental provenance.
- Report failures and nonmonotonic qualification behavior.
- Do not infer physical attenuation from idealized reconstruction alone.
- Keep engineering qualification conditional on stated assumptions.

R5.2 results must remain explicitly identified as synthetic-model
research until supported by independent validation.
